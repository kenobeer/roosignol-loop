How carbon fibre gets into a ski

  1. An old blade comes out of service. This is our proposal with Vattenfall.
  2. Pyrolysis separates the fibres. Carbon fibre survives it.
  3. The recovered carbon fibre is saturated with new resin.
  4. It is frozen as prepreg, ready to use.
  5. It repairs and reinforces skis, for example in season 3.

An honest note. Only a small fraction of a blade is usable carbon fibre, so this complements Vattenfall's other circular solutions and does not replace them. Pyrolysis is energy intensive and a last resort. That is why LOOP first keeps existing skis in use, and only uses recovered fibre where it clearly extends a ski's life.

Source: our interview with Endre Hals, EVI ski factory, Norway.
