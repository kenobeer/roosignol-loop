How carbon fibre gets into a ski

  1. An old blade comes out of service. This is our proposal with Vattenfall.
  2. Pyrolysis separates the fibres. Carbon fibre survives it.
  3. The recovered carbon fibre is saturated with new resin.
  4. It is frozen as prepreg, ready to use.
  5. It repairs and reinforces skis, for example in winter 3.

An honest note. Only a small fraction of a blade is usable carbon fibre, so this complements Vattenfall's other circular solutions and does not replace them. That is why LOOP first keeps existing skis in use, and uses recovered fibre where it clearly extends a ski's life.

Source: our interview with Endre Hals, EVI ski factory, Norway.

The math

Rental skis usually last 3 to 5 winters today (shown at 3). With yearly service and repairs we assume LOOP skis stay in rental for 5 winters, followed by second-hand sale and recycling. Illustrative assumption, to be tested in a pilot.

The math

Rental days = 80 pairs x 20 days x weather factor
Revenue = rental days x your price
Rossignol's fee = rental days x class fee
You keep = revenue minus fee

Your price does not change the number of rental days in this illustration. Illustrative assumptions, to be tested in a pilot.
